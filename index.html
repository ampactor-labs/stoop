THE PRESS

One source, imposed for paper. Change the format and the same pages re-flow onto different sheets.

The PDF is exact: right paper, no margins, nothing for a print dialog to argue with, and a file you can hand to a copy shop. Printing from the browser works too, at 100% scale, margins none, in the orientation the format names. Click any heading or body to edit it; edits save as you type. Fold a numbered test sheet before committing good paper — printers and folding hands vary, and if the numbers come out shuffled, hit SWAP FOLD and test again.

Everything fits on the paper.

HOW TO FOLD (one sheet, one cut)

  1. Hot dog fold. Fold in half the long way, crease hard, unfold.
  2. Eight panels. Fold in half the short way, then fold each outer edge back to the middle crease. Unfold everything.
  3. The cut. Fold in half the short way again and cut along the centre crease from the folded edge, across the middle two panels only. Stop at the quarter creases.
  4. The plus sign. Unfold, fold the long way with the slit along the top, then push both ends toward each other. The slit opens into a diamond, then a four-winged plus.
  5. Collapse. Wrap the wings around the front cover and crease. Eight pages, no staples. Read it once, then leave it somewhere.

Saddle-stitch formats print several sheets instead: print both sides, nest them in order, and staple twice through the spine.

THE SHELF

Streams forget; shelves remember. Every issue is kept whole — its pages, its format, its fold — so a back issue reprints exactly as it shipped rather than as today's draft happens to be set. Export writes a single file that is the issue, the shelf behind it, and a working press for the next one.

SETTINGS & SYNC

Who is writing, and how the notebook travels between your devices.

WHO IS HERE

Everybody who writes for this zine. Entries are stored against a person, not a spelling, so renaming somebody keeps every past piece attached to them — which is also why anyone with work in the archive cannot be removed.

THE ZINE'S NAME

What goes on the cover, the flyer, and the name of every file this press exports. Change it here and the next issue you compile carries it.

THE ADDRESS

Where this scene's issues live, if they live anywhere. The back cover prints it as text and as a code, so somebody who finds the paper can reach the archive. Leave it empty and the paper simply carries no address.

TAKE A PIECE IN

A contributor exports a piece from their own copy and sends you the file; drop it here and it lands in the tray. An exported issue works the same way — importing one puts its back issues on your shelf. Merging the same file twice changes nothing.

CARRY IT ACROSS

There is no server and no account: everything lives in this browser. To share a notebook between two phones or laptops, export here and import there. Merge keeps both sides and prefers the newer copy of anything held by both, so you can each export, swap files, and merge — both devices end up agreeing. Replace overwrites this device entirely.

WHERE YOUR THINGS ARE

Text and lists sit in this browser's local storage; photos sit in its IndexedDB, dithered to 1-bit on the way in so a page-sized image costs tens of kilobytes rather than megabytes. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, because there is nowhere to upload it to. Clearing site data for this page deletes all of it, so export often — the backup file is the only copy that outlives the browser.

START OVER

Start empty clears the sample pieces and everything else, so your first issue is only your own work. Reset to the sample puts the demo notebook back.
