# Folk media

This is the argument stoop was built on. The [README](../README.md) says what the software does; this page says why it exists.

## The argument

Mass media was made by industries for audiences. Social media promised to reverse that. Instead it turned each of its users into a tiny broadcaster, ranked by somebody else's system for keeping people scrolling. Folk media is a third form, made by the people in it for the people in it. It is owned like a commons, small like a scene, periodic like a zine and pointed at the physical world. Folk music stood in the same relation to the record industry.

A zine is a small self-published magazine, usually photocopied and folded by hand. A scene is the group of people who make one together.

Stoop is a working name for a system with that shape. A stoop is the front step of a house, where you sit with your people, facing the street. The name is provisional, and choosing it is an argument to have with the people who will use it. The pitch fits in one sentence: your group chat deserves a zine.

There is no feed anywhere in the system. That absence is deliberate, and the rest of the design rests on it. [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#refusals) opens with that refusal and the others that follow from it.

## Why now

Each point links its source.

- TikTok's recommendation feed has become a national-security question. In January 2026 [TikTok's US operation became a joint venture whose managing investors are Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/22/nx-s1-5685456/nx-s1-5685456), and its recommendation algorithm is to be retrained on US user data under the new owners.
- Much of the open internet is no longer made by people. [An essay on the dead-internet theory](https://medium.com/@ceylinzoi14/is-the-dead-internet-theory-coming-true-the-cost-of-ai-slop-a0b28c1abc42), the idea that most online activity is now automated, collects estimates that bots produce a large share of web traffic and that machines write much of what appears on X and LinkedIn. Being provably human has become scarce, and "everyone here is a vouched human" is now something a product can offer.
- People want the personal web back. [SpaceHey](https://profiletree.com/what-is-myspace/), launched in 2020, rebuilds the classic MySpace experience, with profiles people style in their own HTML and CSS, and it grew steadily on exactly that. Investors have put more than $19 million into [noplace](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/03/noplace-a-mashup-of-twitter-and-myspace-for-gen-z-hits-no-1-on-the-app-store/), an app that mixes Twitter and MySpace for Gen Z.
- Decentralized networks settled who owns an account without giving people a reason to be there. [Mastodon, Bluesky and Nostr each serve millions of users](https://fediview.com/articles/mastodon-vs-bluesky-vs-nostr-2026/), and the protocols under them work. [Free Our Feeds](https://freeourfeeds.com/), a campaign of the Modal Foundation, is raising money for that open infrastructure and building nonprofit governance to keep it out of the hands of a few billionaires.
- [Front Porch Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Porch_Forum), a Vermont neighbourhood digest whose posts human moderators review, has run since 2000 with no anonymity and no endless scrolling. [Cohost](https://tedium.co/2024/09/12/cohost-social-networking-postmortem/), an independent network with no algorithm, announced in September 2024 that it would shut down at the end of the year, after its losses doubled in a single month while its income fell by nearly half. This design takes a lesson from each: an issue is finite, and the press has no server to pay for.
- Community backlash against data centers is growing. [A June Reuters poll cited by Time found only 14% of Americans comfortable with a data center being built nearby, and electricity prices in states with many data centers rose by up to 267% over five years](https://time.com/article/2026/07/22/community-backlash-ai-data-centers/). [Low-tech Magazine's solar-powered website](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/about/the-solar-website/) shows that a real publication can run on 1 to 2.5 watts.
- Governments are pushing young people off social media. [Australia's ban on social media accounts for under-16s](https://www.techpolicy.press/early-lessons-from-australias-teen-social-media-ban-for-the-rest-of-the-world/) took effect on 10 December 2025 as the first nationwide minimum age, and the United Kingdom's consultation on a minimum age also asks about algorithmic restrictions and addictive design features. A system with none of those mechanics gives such rules nothing to keep young people out of.
- Gen Z has already moved much of its social life into group chats on Discord and iMessage, and [Axios reports young people turning to phone-free spaces and digital-detox retreats](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/phone-free-spaces-digital-detox-retreats-gen-z). No tool with a constitution of its own serves that demand yet.
