# The table

What the press looks like and why, decided on 29 September 2026 after a look at the field and at stoop's own screens.

## What the field agrees on

The creative tools people call good converge on five things. Figma's UI3 was built to "center your work rather than the UI": the toolbar went to the bottom, the panels became collapsible, the properties panel was reordered by what people touch most, and hover-only chrome was tried and dropped because it made the workspace unstable. Procreate keeps the art in focus with a minimal interface and makes the tools disappear. Kid Pix's prime directive was no manual, features that explain themselves through use, and a process as satisfying as the picture. Electric Zine Maker's thesis is that "the productivity vibe" scares away people who want to dabble, so a tool must feel like a toy. Canva and Instagram Stories put the controls at the thing you touched, and on a phone as one quick row with more behind it. Paged editors keep a pages strip because the object is pages. Mobile guidance is unanimous about the thumb zone, 44 px targets and bottom sheets. Neo-brutalist chrome works when hierarchy and contrast are kept and fails when every element has the same weight.

## What stoop had

Right in principle: the page is the surface, the name is edited where it is shown, there is no onboarding, the empty cover says what to do in one line, the file's landing reassures ownership, and the identity (cream, ink, riso pink, Anton, Courier) is its own.

Wrong in one structural way, with consequences everywhere: the chrome sat between the maker and the page. The format select, the tool strip, the hint, the photo tray and the inspector all stacked above the pages, so on a laptop the cover started 300 px down and used a third of the width, and on a phone a selected cutting's inspector took more than half the screen. The inspector was a wall of same-weight buttons that wrapped, so nothing had a stable place. There was no way to see the whole zine or reach page 20 of 32 but scrolling. The nav did not run in the order the cycle runs. On a phone the press's fixed strip and inspector sat above the drawers, so the desk's bell was covered while a cutting was selected.

## The table

The press is a paste-up table: the pages in the middle at the largest size the screen allows, a thin rail of tools beside them, the whole zine as a strip of chips above them, and the sheet of controls for a thing appearing beside it when it is touched and going away when the paper is tapped.

The bar carries the zine's name, who is writing, one calm line for the cycle (`№02 · Dee's turn · 7 days to the bell`), and the nav in the order the cycle runs: scraps, desk, press, paper, shelf, then scene, then hand it on.

The rail holds the add tools and undo. The table's head holds the format, the generation, the fit meter and the zoom (fit a spread, fit a page, 100%). The strip has a chip a page: click one and the table goes there; drag one onto another and the page moves there, its cuttings and its piece with it, covers pinned. The sheet is a docked column on a laptop and a bottom sheet on a phone that never takes more than 45% of the screen.

On a phone the five stops of the cycle are a tab bar in the thumb zone, the rail is a row above it, and a drawer opens above every piece of press chrome, so the bell is always reachable.

Nothing about the model, the file, the format or the suites' selectors changed to get here; it is structure, position, grouping and words.
